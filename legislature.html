State Senator | Wyoming State Legislature, Cheyenne, WY (1/11–)
Senate Minority Leader | Wyoming State Legislature, Cheyenne, WY (1/13–1/25)

In November 2010, I was elected to serve as Wyoming State Senator for Senate District 9 in Laramie, WY, as a member of the 61st Wyoming Legislature. I was further selected as Senate Minority Leader for the 62nd, 63rd, 64th, 65th, 66th and 67th Legislative Sessions. Of the bills I sponsored, 16 have become law, and so have 79 that I cosponsored.

As Senate Minority Leader, I coordinated caucus policy positions, led floor debate, and represented the minority caucus in negotiations with legislative leadership, the Governor, and executive branch agencies. In this role, I was directly involved in the passage of legislation and the development of Wyoming's biennial state budget exceeding $9 billion.

Blockchain & Digital Assets

Chairman, Blockchain Task Force (2018–2019)
Chairman, Select Committee on Blockchain, Financial Technology & Digital Innovation Technology (2020–)

As creator and co-Chair of the Wyoming Blockchain Task Force and its successor Select Committee, I have led Wyoming's transformation into a globally recognized leader in digital asset governance. Through these bodies, I helped guide the successful passage of more than 40 bills establishing clear legal frameworks for decentralized autonomous organizations, decentralized unincorporated nonprofit associations, digital asset custodial services, special purpose depository institutions, financial technology sandboxes, utility tokens, digital asset commercial law, and the Wyoming Stable Token Act. The DAO and DUNA laws were each the first of their kind. The Select Committee continues to refine and expand Wyoming's digital asset laws in response to technological change and evolving federal regulatory developments. In collaboration with stakeholders across state government, industry, and academia, the committee's work has supported first-of-their-kind initiatives such as Wyoming's state-backed stable token program and provided sustained legislative study and oversight of digital innovation policy.

Digital Information Privacy

Chairman, Taskforce on Digital Information Privacy (2014–16)

With the passage of 2014 Senate File 91, I created and chaired a seven-member taskforce of legislators and gubernatorial appointees charged with modernizing Wyoming's statutory framework for digital information privacy. The taskforce conducted a comprehensive review of state law and led to the passage of multiple bills addressing the collection, use, and protection of personally identifiable information, student data privacy, genetic information privacy, and law enforcement body-worn camera data. This work established Wyoming as an early leader among states in addressing digital privacy challenges through statute.

Education

Ranking Member, Senate Education Committee (2011–); Select Committee on School Finance Recalibration (2015, 2017, 2020, 2025); Select Committee on Statewide Education Accountability (2011–16); Select Committee on School Facilities (2011–); Wyoming's Tomorrow Task Force (2019–2020)

For fifteen years, I have been deeply engaged in crafting Wyoming's education policy and budgets as a member of every education-related legislative committee assembled during that period. Key accomplishments include helping design and refine the Wyoming Accountability in Education Act, participation in four statewide school finance model recalibrations culminating in the 2026 recalibration (SF 81), which I led through the select committee and both chambers and which became the first recalibration bill to pass in 15 years, adding about $114 million a year and raising model teacher salaries 20.7 percent, a significant role in school facilities funding totaling billions of dollars, co-sponsorship of the Wyoming Safe2Tell program, passage of Computer Science for All, statutory work supporting statewide reading literacy and early intervention efforts, and Wyoming's consistently above-average NAEP performance.

Minerals, Business & Economic Development

Ranking Member, Senate Minerals, Business and Economic Development Committee (2011–); Subcommittee on Municipal Solid Waste (2012); Task Force on Nuclear Energy Production (2011–12); Select Committee on the Wyoming Value Added Energy and Industrial Plan (2014–16); Select Committee on Coal/Mineral Bankruptcies (2019–)

As the longest continuously serving member of the Senate Minerals, Business and Economic Development Committee, I have focused on responsible mineral development, statewide economic diversification, and environmental protection. In each of the following I played a significant role: the ENDOW economic development initiative, NRC Agreement State authorization, Wyoming solid waste law including cease and transfer and remediation programs, creation of the Wyoming Energy Authority, the sage grouse impact mitigation credit program, revisions to oil and gas bonding, forced pooling, and unitization statutes, and coal reclamation bonding. I led the 2025 small operator oil and gas bonding pool (SF 20) and the 2025 carbon dioxide enhanced oil recovery incentive (SF 17), which aligns state policy with the federal 45Q tax credit.

Management Council

Ranking Member, Management Council (2013–2024)

The Management Council serves as the governing board of directors for the Wyoming Legislature, with responsibility for the operation, administration, and strategic oversight of the legislative branch and the Legislative Service Office. During my service, I participated in setting legislative branch policy and priorities, directing and overseeing the Legislative Service Office's legal, research, fiscal, and administrative functions, approving interim committee structure and budgets, monitoring interim committee activity, conducting administrative rule review, and approving personnel actions and the operating budget for the legislative branch.

Capitol Restoration

Ranking Member, Capitol Building Restoration Oversight Group (2014–2019)

I served on the Capitol Building Restoration Oversight Group for the full duration of the Capitol Square Project, a multi-year, approximately $300 million restoration and expansion of the Wyoming State Capitol complex. Comprised of the Governor, eight legislators, and, from 2017, the State Treasurer, the Oversight Group provided governance and fiduciary oversight for project scope, schedule, and budget, coordinating across branches of government to ensure accountability. The project was completed in 2019 within its authorized budget of about $300 million and in accordance with historic preservation and functional modernization goals.
