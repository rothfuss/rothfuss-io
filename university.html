I have been an instructor with the University of Wyoming Honors College (formerly the Honors Program) since 2007. During this time, I have taught more than thirty Honors College courses, as well as undergraduate and graduate courses across Global and Area Studies and the College of Engineering. My teaching emphasizes experiential learning, interdisciplinary inquiry, and the application of technical, scientific, and policy concepts to real-world challenges. A detailed teaching record is provided in the Teaching Experience section below.

I currently serve as Director of Internships, Research, and Entrepreneurship for the Honors College, where I lead the development and coordination of high-impact experiential learning opportunities for Honors students. In this role, I work closely with students, faculty, and external partners to identify, design, and support mentored research and internship experiences that align with student goals and Honors learning outcomes. Since 2023, I have facilitated more than 120 paid research and internship placements for Honors students.

I served as Director, University of Wyoming Summer High School Institute (HSI) from 2016 to 2021. The Summer HSI is a competitive, three-week residential program that brings high-achieving rising high school juniors from across Wyoming to the UW campus for an immersive academic experience. The program combines rigorous honors-style coursework with residential life, leadership development, and cross-disciplinary engagement, and has been a significant pipeline for recruiting students into the Honors College and the University of Wyoming more broadly.

I chaired the Honors College Steering Committee (2016), leading the committee responsible for guiding the transition of the University of Wyoming Honors Program into the University of Wyoming Honors College and providing leadership in the development of the academic vision, governance structure, and institutional framework that established the Honors College as a distinct academic unit.

Service to the University includes serving as Chairman, Honors College Steering Committee (2016); member, College of Education Visioning Group (2018); member, UW Director of Forensics Hiring Committee (2019, 2025); member, Entrepreneurship Working Group (2019); member, Honors Major Committee (2021–2023); member, University Studies Program “V” Committee (2022–2023); and member, Honors College Concentration Course Review Committee (2024–present).

Courses Taught

The Future of Nanotechnology: Social, Political, and Economic Consequences | University of Wyoming (2007–) – This senior level Honors College course provides a broad examination of the development of nanotechnology. I designed the course to be multidisciplinary and to examine a rapidly advancing field from scientific, social, political, and economic perspectives. Through the course I teach students the physics of novel and emerging technologies, including overviews of quantum physics, semiconductors, and photonics, while situating these technologies in a broader societal context and considering how continued advances in nanoscale science are likely to shape future technological capabilities, economic structures, and policy choices.

Diplomacy and Negotiations | University of Wyoming (2008–) – This upper division course focuses on the development and practical application of diplomacy and negotiation skills, with extensive use of real world role-playing scenarios. Students learn negotiation theory and techniques, and apply them through simulated bilateral and multilateral negotiation exercises. Students also learn how to operate in a diplomatic setting and as part of a delegation. This course is primarily experiential and generally proves to be stimulating and exciting for the participants. It has been offered through the UW Honors College, the International Studies Program and the Political Science Department.

Innovate Wyoming | University of Wyoming (2023) – This senior level multidisciplinary Honors College course was co-designed and taught with Dr. Penelope Shihab in collaboration with the Wyoming Center for Entrepreneurship and Innovation. The course emphasized experiential learning through statewide travel, visits to Wyoming businesses and startups, and direct engagement with entrepreneurs, innovators, and community colleges. Students worked in interdisciplinary teams to identify real challenges facing Wyoming communities and industries, apply open innovation and entrepreneurial frameworks, and develop and present feasible solutions grounded in market and feasibility analysis.

Ideation, Innovation and Invention | University of Wyoming (2020, 2022, 2024) – This senior level multidisciplinary Honors College course introduces students to open innovation through experiential, project based work focused on real world challenges. Students work in interdisciplinary teams to define problems, apply structured ideation techniques, develop and refine solutions and minimum viable prototypes, and assess feasibility and pathways to adoption or invention disclosure. Students are trained in and have access to the University of Wyoming Maker Space as a prototyping resource. The course is highly experiential and student interest driven, emphasizing iterative design and engagement with industry, community, and emerging technology contexts.

Graduate Thermodynamics | University of Wyoming (2013) – This graduate level chemical and petroleum engineering course focuses on multiphase, multicomponent equilibria. The curriculum was designed to examine molecular thermodynamics of pure materials and mixtures, specifically covering phase equilibria and the application of equations of state. Key topics included classical thermodynamics of phase equilibria, intermolecular forces, corresponding states, and detailed analyses of fugacities in gas and liquid mixtures. The course also covered specialized systems such as polymer, electrolyte, gas, and solid solutions, preparing students for advanced research and industry applications.

Thermodynamics | University of Wyoming (2010) – This core engineering science course introduces undergraduate engineering students to core thermodynamic concepts and principles.

Unit Operations (TA) | University of Washington (2000–2002) – I served as a teaching assistant for the Chemical Engineering junior level Unit Operations course for 3 years. As a course TA, I was responsible for teaching and laboratory oversight for one section of students, and oversight of all students for the experiments for which I had responsibility (e.g. ultra-high vacuum transport).

Introduction to Engineering (TA) | University of Wyoming (1995) – I served as a teaching assistant for ES1060 – Introduction to Engineering which included laboratory support, grading and some teaching.
